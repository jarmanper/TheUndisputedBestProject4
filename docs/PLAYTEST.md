# Playtest results

Real tester feedback only. Never add anything here that did not happen (AGENTS.md rule 7).

## Round 1 — Web build, Oct 28 2026

Two testers, both playing the GitHub Pages web export. Neither finished the game.

### Blockers (nobody got past the opening)

| # | What happened | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | The link "took too long and never fully loaded the game." | Tester 1 |
| B2 | Loaded after a long wait, then "it would just hang and I was unable to play after pressing **New Game**." | Jadin Hutchinson |
| B3 | "The game kept glitching when approached so I was unable to get past the 2nd set of words." | Tester 1 |

**Neither tester reached the twin, the clock workshop, or any beat past the opening corridor.** Every story reaction below is based on the first few rooms only.

### What they thought the story was

Asked to retell it in two sentences:

- Tester 1: "There was an outbreak at a hospital or mental hospital, and you are trying to get out without being hurt."
- Jadin: "There was some type of outbreak inside a hospital or mental facility that caused everything to go wrong. The main goal seems to be finding a way to escape while avoiding the infected people."

**Both read it as a generic zombie-outbreak escape.** Neither mentioned the missing parents, the twin brother, the clock motif, or searching for anything. The actual premise — you are looking for what happened to your parents — did not come across at all. The player goal they inferred (*escape*) is the opposite of the real one (*go deeper*).

### What the environment did tell them (this part worked)

Both independently reported the same read, without being told to look for it:

- Tester 1: "It is not abandoned as there is power and the zombie like things were coming from almost every room."
- Jadin: "The building still had electricity, and there were infected people coming out of different rooms. This made me think that whatever happened was fairly recent and that the hospital wasn't completely abandoned."

Power-still-on plus creatures-in-every-room is legible environmental storytelling. It is the one "Found" moment that landed.

### The miss

- Both tried to **interact with the computer terminals** expecting hints or progress. Nothing happened. Jadin: "I tried interacting with some of the computers because I thought they might give me clues or help move the story forward, but nothing really happened."
- Tester 1: "more clues or contextual hints could be apparent... I was relying on the words."
- Jadin: "having more environmental clues and interactive objects would make it easier to follow without depending so much on the text."

**Both testers asked for less dependence on text and more story in the world** — which is exactly the assignment's "Found" theme.

### Implications

1. **The web export is the top priority.** If a grader cannot load it or it hangs on New Game, nothing else is assessed. B1/B2 are a grading blocker.
2. **The premise is not reaching the player in the opening minutes.** Another team grades the story. The parents, and the fact that the player is searching rather than escaping, need to be established in the first room, in the world rather than in text.
3. **`computer_terminal` reads as interactive and is not.** Either make the terminals do something or stop making them look usable. The same affordance question applies to any prop a player walks up to and presses E on.
4. B3 ("glitching when approached") is unexplained and needs a repro before it can be fixed.
